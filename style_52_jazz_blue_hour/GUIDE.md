# Jazz Piano Lounge · Blue Hour

Ink blue and amber pools of light make a smart, relaxed music room that still welcomes breakfast.

Combination: Jazz Piano Lounge + Parisian Contemporary + Contemporary Art Deco. A residential music-room proposal with subtle Deco accents.

## Main elements

- Materials: ink blue velvet; oatmeal linen; smoked oak; amber glass; aged brass.
- Living and dining: small ink-blue 180cm rounded two-seat sofa; one oatmeal linen 80cm swivel chair; oval smoked-oak 85x45cm coffee table; slim smoked-oak TV cabinet and discreet TV; 100cm round smoked-oak dining table and FOUR oatmeal chairs; compact ebony Kawai NV12 hybrid grand piano with bench; amber-glass table lamp; brass reading lamp; single stepped silver-bronze Deco mirror; two small framed black-and-white portraits of Bill Evans and Nina Simone; one playful ochre abstract print.
- Bedroom: ink-blue upholstered queen; smoked oak nightstands; amber glass bedside lamps; oatmeal bedding.
- Solarium: black metal and blue outdoor cushions; amber portable lamps; round outdoor dining set.

## Implementation

1. Use an ink-blue fine-herringbone wallpaper accent at the piano and warm ivory elsewhere; add one ochre pillow and an amber lamp, with a reading chair angled toward music.
2. Begin with upholstery and timber samples. Repeat the selected sofa, chair, tables, lighting and art across all three living/dining views.
3. Maintain a separate everyday dining table for four. Choose compact chairs that tuck in; keep the route to the kitchen and bedroom doors open.
4. Use a queen mattress 152.4 x 203.2 cm and two compact nightstands with two lamps. Carry the material family into bedding and one artwork.
5. Choose outdoor-rated frames and cushions for the solarium; keep its black tile, wood ceiling, glazing and black structural pillar. Use plants and movable lamps for atmosphere.
6. Layer warm lamps with a usable task light over the dining table. Buy lamps and sample textiles first, then larger furniture. Avoid purchasing an entire matching suite.

## Piano, everyday use and lighting

The supplied Kawai NV12 references show an ebony-polish, short-body hybrid with a grand-style opening lid: 151.5 cm wide x 87 cm deep, 102.5 cm high closed/music rest flat and 172.5 cm with the lid fully open; 173 kg. Confirmed against [Kawai's NV12 specifications](https://www.kawai-global.com/product/nv12/). Keep this shallow body; a normal acoustic grand is substantially different and is not the design target.

Proposed zone: piano along the solid living-room wall, toward the kitchen end of that wall but clear of the refrigerator. Keyboard faces into the room; the 87 cm body depth runs toward the wall. Use the short lid prop in the concepts. Reserve approximately 90 cm beyond the keyboard for bench and playing movement, plus a separate circulation route. This is a planning allowance, not a measured fit certificate; tape out the 151.5 x 87 cm body and bench area before ordering. Do not use the solarium for the instrument. Keep drinks on the side tables, not on the piano.

Seat two on the 180 cm sofa and one in the armchair; a dining chair can join when a fourth guest listens. The 100 cm dining table remains a real eating/work surface with four chairs. Keep the small coffee table movable. A shallow closed cabinet accommodates scores, remotes and household clutter. The TV stays modest and secondary, beside the piano zone rather than above its opening lid.

Use warm dimmable bulbs, approximately 2700 K as a starting point, in a shaded floor lamp, a low table lamp and a small dining pendant. Place a task light so the score is readable without glare. Evening concepts should show pools of amber light and readable faces/materials, not a black room. Daytime remains useful for work and meals. The piano's headphone capability supports quieter practice; rugs and curtains soften room reflections but do not soundproof it.

## Residential cost discipline

The piano is the centerpiece and separate from the furnishing budget. Spend on a supportive chair, comfortable compact sofa and good dimmable lamps. Save with a walnut-veneer cabinet, ready-made rug, framed authorized reproduction prints and one ready-made stepped mirror. Use woven-look wallpaper on one wall only. Skip custom bars, stone wall cladding, built-in booths and elaborate ceiling work. These are cost-control choices, not price quotes.

The stepped mirror is inspired by the background of the supplied reference photo, reduced to one condo-scale piece. Do not reproduce the people or venue. Jazz portraits should be purchased as licensed prints; generated portraits are conceptual placement examples, not archival photographs.

## Watch-outs

Avoid nightclub darkness, blue LEDs, oversized art collections and high-cost built-in cabinetry. Preserve all existing openings, floors, kitchen geometry and 2.7 m ceiling. Renders are furnishing concepts; small image-generation variations are not approved construction changes. Verify furniture and circulation in the measured model before purchase.
